How to play

The goal: destroy enemy ships — and become Master of the Universe by placing a star base in every stellar system. (Condensed from the original 1979 Alto Trek manual.)

  • Steering & firing direction — the Direction circle (see the colored guide overlay): click the green outer ring to point your ship, the narrow orange inner ring to aim your phasers (the clickable band is small — aim for the orange). The big dot on the rim is your heading; the small dot is phaser aim.
  • Moving — click in the Accel bar to set thrust (it stays on; click its bottom to cut it). There's inertia: to slow down, turn around and thrust the other way. Don't drift idle — gravity pulls you into stars.
  • Shields — put the cursor just beside your ship in the big lower scanner: left-click raises a shield level (hold for max), right-click lowers. Shields drain energy; energy is life.
  • Firing — the FIRE Phasers / FIRE Torpedoes boxes. Torpedoes fly where the ship points, phasers where the phaser aim points.
  • Jumping between systems — get near a star gate (the + in the scanners) and slow to a stop. Click the top-left scanner to see the Galaxy map, note the direction of your destination system, point your ship that way, and accelerate — the screen flashes when you jump. (Right-click the Galaxy map to return to the local scan.)
  • Bases — press Drop base (sends ESC B) to deploy one; you carry a limited supply. Bases dropped near asteroid clusters become constructor bases that manufacture more. Approach one of your bases slowly to dock: full repair and resupply. In the Galaxy map, left-click a system containing your base to jump to it; middle-click (two-finger tap) for a remote scan around it.
  • Chat — click the screen, then type: your message broadcasts to every player (and reveals your race and location…).
  • Touch screens — tap = left click, long-press = right click (held until you lift), drag = hold left while moving, two-finger tap = middle click, pinch = zoom.
  • If you die you'll usually "rise to fight again" — unless you ran completely out of energy, so watch the Energy bar.
About

Alto Trek was written by Gene Ball and Rick Rashid at the University of Rochester in the late 1970s for the Xerox Alto — one of the first networked multiplayer games ever made. There is no server-side game logic: exactly as in 1978, every ship broadcasts its state onto the shared (now virtual) Ethernet.

Emulation by ContrAltoJS (AGPL-3.0), a JavaScript port of the Living Computer Museum's ContrAlto. Source for this site (including its WebSocket networking changes): github.com/erickball/alto-trek.
